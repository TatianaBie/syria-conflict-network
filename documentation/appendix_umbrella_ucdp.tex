Armed groups in Syria frequently operate under shifting labels, split into
factions, or fight as part of a larger coalition, so that the same organisation
appears under several names and closely related factions are recorded as
separate actors. Both source datasets therefore have to be harmonised onto this
common list of actors before they can be merged.

\subsubsection*{UCDP}

The UCDP data are comparatively clean in this respect: the Syrian sample
contains $97$ distinct actor labels. Of these, $17$ are dropped because they
refer to actors that are marginal in the Syrian conflict or that we do not
follow (for instance the Government of Jordan, the PKK, or the generic labels
``Opponents of al-Assad'' and ``Supporters of al-Assad''); together they account
for a negligible share of the events. The remaining $80$ labels are mapped onto
the actors of interest as reported in Table~\ref{tab:umbrella_ucdp}. An actor is
either \emph{kept} as a formation in its own right (38 labels), in which case the
umbrella name is only a standardised version of its own name, or it is
\emph{merged} into a larger formation (42 labels), in which case all of its events
are attributed to the umbrella group. A few labels refer to joint operations
rooms or alliances of several of our actors; their events are attributed to each
of the formations listed in the last column.

\subsubsection*{ACLED}

The ACLED data are recorded at a much more granular level: the Syrian sample
contains $757$ distinct actor names in the two main actor fields, and $925$ once
the associated-actor fields are taken into account. Small local brigades,
military councils and operations rooms are listed individually, and the same
formation often appears under several spellings. We therefore apply the same
type of renaming to ACLED, mapping its actor labels onto the $36$ actors of
interest listed above; the correspondence is reported in
Table~\ref{tab:umbrella_acled}.
